I am done with agentic coding, not because the **LLMs aren't good enough**, but because **I am not good enough**. I am not ready to have a computer think for me, and me, the person writing in this moment, doesn't know when I will be.

## What this is not.
I don't need to discuss the merits or advancements of large language models, anyone presently reading this knows exactly why I'm feeling the way I feel. It is unnavoidable, everyone who considers themselves a programmer has had to go through this at some point in the last 6-12 months.

I also don't really need to discuss the ethics, we all know how many fucks programmers give about those. Neither the spiritual implications of enjoying art produced by an algorithm, or the nature of offloading our thinking to a model built by a company that would sell us the air we breath if it'd make back the money they currently owe investors.

I don't even want to discuss skill atrophy, we've been letting our skills atrophy for years, there are always new skills to hone. Yet we continue on and still do hard things and break new records and make new strides. At the end of the day who cares if our programming skills atrophy when the models could become so good that no one ever needs to program again.

**I'm not here for that**, they're all arguments that have merit, they can all be said, and dismissed, or dismantled. These are not why I cannot let go of programming for programmings sake.

## What got me here.
When I first started programming, I struggled, it took about 6 months of working full time for it to feel like I was making progress. I was straight out of sixthform (high school if you're in the US) and had somehow managed to land myself a job programming full time with very little experience. Once I overcame that hurdle though, things started to flow, every day in the saddle was another step closer to compitence. I started to stay late, not for money or for appearances, but because I couldn't take the codebase home with me. I read books, I studied the codebase, I experimented, build, tinkered and revelled in the bad neighbourhood that is a banks finance engine.

After 6 years of programming, around 2019, the joy of it left me, I started to program to live, and lost my appetite for complexity. At a similar time I was getting more heavily into weed. I'd finish work and rush home to smoke a joint, then mong out on the sofa to the latest playstation game I was obsessed with. In work I was a present, warm boddied, and vaguely some place else at all times. I would do what I could already do well, but was unwilling to learn anything new. I began to coast, only picking up what was required of the job.

I continued like this through covid, all the way up until my son made his entrance. Its hard to stay despondent and in a haze with another life so reliant on you. His arrival meant it was over for me and weed. We'd had a...run, but it was time to let go. It still took all the effort I could muster to give up, but as the fog cleared from my head, I finally began to thirst for complexity and learning again. Once again programming became my focus and obsession.

At first it was a program called Picotron. I started making a game for it and then fell into the rabbithole that is games programming and development. Then it was a shell, Swell, which I am still working on to this day. Then it was a OS kernel, more games development, linux ricing, many different programming languages, platforms and tools, and finally an open source contribution to the Odin programming language to enable to development of games in Odin on the Playdate.

## Think less tokens
At the start of that little process, I wasn't using AI at all, but gradually over time it ramped up, and by the time I contributed to the Odin programming language, I essentially had to lean all my weight on the the models to achieve what I wanted to. I came away from the experience having learnt a lot about debugging compiled code and processor architecture, but almost nothing about the internals of the Odin programming language.

It hadn't quite dawned on me yet, that the thing that I loved most about programming, the thing I had fallen in love with, was being taken away by the offloading of thought and understanding. Until last week. The events aren't relevant, but essentially I was confronted by my bias and decided that I needed to understand why I felt so at odds with what programming has become. 

See, it all comes back to my early experiences of programming, time in the saddle, every day on the job is an opportunity to advance. there is always room for improvement, and without time spent in front of our editor, thinking deep and hard about the system in front of us, we cannot progress.

If AI had come along when I was in my despondent phase, thinking only about getting through the next day so I could do it all again, it would have found me at the right time. A tool that can do my thinking for me, that can help me achieve the next objective, and build the next thing, while my brain and skills continue to attrophy under the weight of the cali weed I was smoking every day. Sign me up, 200$ a month.

## Digital Minimalism
At the same time as this, I was going through a break up with social media, the genocide in Palestine was causing me huge anxiety and I while I desperately scrolled reddit looking for more news, without realising it, my contribution to the attention economy was racking up.

I decided I needed a break, but continued to watch youtube videos, and one day while watching a video about ipod mods of all things I came across digital minimalism by Cal Newport.
